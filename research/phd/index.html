Infinite-Dimensional Hamiltonian Systems and Their Invariant Measures

My dissertation develops a functional-analytic approach to infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian systems based on invariant measures in separable Hilbert spaces. It studies translation-invariant Banach measures, symplectically invariant measures, Koopman representations of Hamiltonian flows, and continuation of dynamics through singularity formation.

Official title: «Бесконечномерные гамильтоновы системы и их инвариантные меры» · Specialty: 1.1.1 Real, Complex and Functional Analysis · Supervisor: Yuri N. Orlov, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, Associate Professor.

Why this matters

Why it matters for mathematical physics

Many evolution equations in mathematical physics, including nonlinear wave and Schrödinger equations, have infinitely many degrees of freedom. The usual finite-dimensional notion of volume has no direct translation-invariant counterpart in their phase spaces. This makes invariant measures and statistical descriptions harder to construct.

A framework for dynamics

The dissertation constructs invariant measures for classes of Hamiltonian flows and uses them to study the evolution of observables through Koopman operators. This provides a mathematical route to spectral and statistical analysis in model infinite-dimensional systems.

Understanding singularities

When a solution grows without bound in finite time, the original phase space may no longer contain its trajectory. Phase-space extensions and continued flows allow the behavior around and beyond that event to be studied in the models treated here.

These are theoretical tools and model results; their use for a particular physical equation requires checking its assumptions. See the author’s abstract for the precise statements.

Research line

Main questions

Invariant measures and Hamiltonian flows

Study of measures on Hilbert spaces that remain invariant under infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian dynamics, and of the structural assumptions under which such invariant descriptions can be constructed.

Operator representations

Koopman-type representations provide a linear operator viewpoint on nonlinear Hamiltonian evolution. This connects the dynamics of observables with functional-analytic structure in infinite-dimensional spaces.

Singular evolution

A second line concerns trajectories that reach singular configurations in finite time and the mathematical problem of continuing dynamics through the singularity rather than terminating the solution concept there.

Phase-space extensions

For systems with exploding or singular solutions, extended phase spaces can provide a framework in which the evolution remains mathematically describable beyond the original singular event.
