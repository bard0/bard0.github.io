Research profile

Applied mathematician with completed research projects on finite-step SGD and spectral reliability for nonstationary time series. The common theme is stability: when a discrete stochastic optimizer becomes unstable, and when a local spectral estimate should or should not be trusted from finite data. Background in functional analysis, measure theory, operator methods, and dynamical systems.

Finite-Step Stochastic Dynamics of SGD — completed v1.0

  • Exact finite-step analysis of SGD stability without reducing the dynamics to a Brownian or Langevin approximation.
  • Explicit finite-sum examples where zero-order local descriptors agree but exact stability thresholds differ.
  • Solvable stability relation q=(1−ηλ)²+η²GΣ with ηc=2λ/(λ²+GΣ).
  • Controlled matched-system comparisons: approximately 85% of stability-threshold differences and 98% of matched sharpness-gap differences explained by the noise-geometry model.
  • Matched controls, held-out evaluation, bootstrap/permutation inference, negative controls, and resumable checkpoints.
  • Preregistered higher-order neural transfer test retained as a negative result after failing cross-regime robustness gates rather than being rescued post hoc.

Nonstationary Time Series & Spectral Reliability — completed case study

  • Started from causal adaptive-history selection for local Koopman/EDMD models; the first Lepski-style selector was too conservative and no generic adaptive-window advantage was established across the broad benchmark suite.
  • Moved from mode-wise matching to invariant-subspace geometry and showed that cross-scale slow-subspace disagreement predicts estimation error in controlled regular-gap systems (AUROC about 0.79; about 0.83 for the fixed-outer-gap cluster benchmark).
  • Showed that the same cross-scale signal is not a reliable direct measure of true dynamical drift.
  • Non-normal tests exposed an object-existence failure mode: the selected real/conjugation-closed spectral cluster can cease to be admissible before conditional survivor error becomes large.
  • Derived and validated a projected boundary-complexification probability law (MAE about 0.00225 over 252 fresh conditions) and studied the effect of spectral-center uncertainty on data-level risk estimates.
  • Final matched-overlap tests found that reliability prediction can remain informative even when estimation noise and smooth true drift are not causally separable from the tested observables.
