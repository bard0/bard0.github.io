What I am working on now

Most of my current work grows out of one idea: treat learning algorithms as dynamical systems, not only as optimization procedures. That is what led me from operator theory and infinite-dimensional dynamics to finite-step questions in SGD.

Current focus

Finite-Step Stochastic Dynamics of SGD

The question I keep coming back to is simple to state: what information about stochastic gradients is actually enough to predict whether finite-step SGD is stable? This becomes especially important near Edge of Stability, where a continuous-time approximation can smooth away the discrete effects that control the algorithm.

In a controlled finite-sum construction, I can keep the population Hessian and the local gradient-noise covariance fixed at a reference point and still change the exact second-moment stability threshold. In other words, curvature plus zero-order covariance is not always a complete local description.

q = (1 − ηλ)² + η²GΣ
ηc = 2λ / (λ² + GΣ)

Here GΣ describes how the geometry of the noise changes with the state. In the solvable model it shifts the critical learning rate directly. The interesting part for me is that two systems that look identical under familiar local descriptors can still behave differently at a finite step size.

Mathematical background

Infinite-Dimensional Dynamics & Mathematical Physics

Before moving into SGD, I worked mostly on questions from functional analysis and mathematical physics: invariant measures, Hamiltonian flows, singular dynamics and operator representations in infinite-dimensional spaces. That background still shapes how I think about learning dynamics today.

  • Invariant measures.
    Measures on Hilbert spaces that stay invariant under Hamiltonian flows.
  • Singular dynamics.
    How trajectories of differential equations can be continued through singularity formation.
  • Operator methods.
    Koopman representations for infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian flows.
  • Functional analysis.
    Banach limits and finitely additive measures on L∞(R).
