Current research

I currently work on two related problems: how discrete stochastic optimization behaves near its stability boundary, and how much trust to put in a spectrum estimated from a nonstationary time series.

Current project

Finite-Step Stochastic Dynamics of SGD

I treat SGD as a discrete stochastic dynamical system. Near the Edge of Stability, finite step size can produce behavior that is absent from diffusion approximations.

In a controlled finite-sum model, two systems have the same population Hessian and the same local gradient-noise covariance at a reference point, but different exact second-moment stability thresholds.

q = (1 − ηλ)² + η²GΣ
ηc = 2λ / (λ² + GΣ)

The term GΣ captures how noise geometry changes with the state and enters the exact critical-step formula. It separates systems that are indistinguishable under standard local descriptors.

Current project

Causal Spectral Reliability for Nonstationary Time Series

When a local Koopman/EDMD spectrum moves, the first question is whether the dynamics actually changed or the estimate simply became unstable because there is too little data. That is the problem I am working on.

I compare slow invariant subspaces across different history lengths and across time, using only past observations. Spectral gaps and non-normality are tracked explicitly because both can make an apparently moving spectrum hard to interpret.

The project began with adaptive history windows and narrowed to the more basic reliability question. In controlled operator tests, global eigenvector conditioning was not enough to predict whether the selected slow spectral object remained well defined: the placement of non-normal coupling relative to the spectral boundary mattered.
