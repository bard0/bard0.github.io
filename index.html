Vladimir Glazatov

My background is in functional analysis and infinite-dimensional dynamical systems. Alongside my PhD research in mathematical physics, I study finite-step stability of stochastic gradient descent and the reliability of Koopman/EDMD spectral estimates from nonstationary time series.

My PhD studies span 2022–2026; the dissertation defense is forthcoming. I am available for postdoctoral and research roles in mathematics and theoretical machine learning.

Current research interests: SGD stability and stochastic optimization; spectral methods for nonstationary time series; infinite-dimensional dynamics and mathematical physics; neural dynamics and adaptive neural interfaces.

Independent research project

Finite-Step Stochastic Dynamics of SGD

Scalar and trace-level noise approximations can miss finite-step mean-square stability boundaries. An exact finite-sum model and matched numerical comparisons isolate the role of state-dependent gradient-noise covariance geometry in stability thresholds and sharpness gaps.

Independent research case study

Spectral Reliability for Nonstationary Time Series

Local Koopman/EDMD estimates from short trajectories can yield unstable spectral modes, particularly under non-normal perturbations. This case study quantifies admissibility boundaries, relates cross-scale Grassmann distance to invariant-subspace error, and calibrates risk intervals for selected spectral clusters.

PhD dissertation · defense forthcoming

Infinite-Dimensional Hamiltonian Systems and Their Invariant Measures

How can mathematical physics describe systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom? My dissertation introduces measures invariant under the flows of a broad class of infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian systems, together with methods for studying and continuing dynamics through singularities.
