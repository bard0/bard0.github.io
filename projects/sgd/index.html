Finite-Step Stochastic Dynamics of SGD

I started this project by asking whether minibatch noise can shift the effective dynamics of SGD. After several controls and failed hypotheses, the question became more precise: what stochastic information is actually needed to predict a finite-step stability or dynamical quantity? The final version combines exact constructions, controlled numerical tests, and one preregistered neural test that did not support the stronger hypothesis.

Research question

How the question changed

At first I treated stochasticity as a perturbation of local deterministic dynamics. The controls quickly showed that this picture was too simple. By the end, the useful question was no longer “what is the next correction term?” but “which stochastic descriptors are enough for a particular finite-step quantity?”

(Pηf)(θ) = E[f(θ − ηgB(θ))]

Research path

What survived the tests

1. Controlled spectral perturbationIn a stochastic Duffing testbed, the perturbative predictor tracked measured spectral shifts very closely when the assumptions were enforced.
2. Additive-noise falsificationZero-mean additive noise did not provide a generic physical mean spectral-shift mechanism. Noise amplitude alone was not enough.
3. Temporal covariance hypothesisThe proposed centered temporal-covariance “memory” mechanism did not survive the filtration check for ordinary iid / with-replacement SGD.
4. State-dependent noise geometryAn exact scalar finite-sum construction showed that lower-order local descriptors can match while finite-step stability changes.
5. Higher-order neural transferThe mixed fourth derivative was measurable and gave a small pooled gain, but it failed the cross-regime criterion fixed before the final test.
6. Final questionThe project ended up as a study of descriptor sufficiency: what information is required for a specified finite-step SGD target?
Analytical result

Low-order local descriptors can be insufficient

For an exactly solvable scalar finite-sum model, state-dependent stochastic geometry enters the exact second-moment stability boundary:

q = (1 − ηλ)² + η²GΣ
ηc = 2λ / (λ² + GΣ)

The point is specific: matching the lower-order reference geometry does not, in general, fix the finite-step stability target. I do not claim multiplicative noise or mean-square stability theory themselves as new.

Why the matched comparisons matter

The simpler projected-noise baseline already explains most absolute sharpness variation. The stronger evidence for state-dependent noise geometry appears when systems are matched on simpler descriptors and the target is the difference between them.

So the claim is narrower than “noise geometry explains SGD”: the extra information matters in these matched finite-step comparisons.

Negative result

A higher-order neural descriptor did not pass the robustness gate

The neural branch tested χmix = D⁴L[u,u,v,v] against the strongest frozen lower-order baseline using grouped held-out evaluation.

Result: the preregistered regime-robust incremental-value claim failed. I kept the difference between learning-rate regimes as an exploratory observation rather than redefining the hypothesis after the fact.

Literature boundary

Positioning

The project is positioned relative to 2026 work on Edge-of-Stability dynamics and finite-step SGD beyond Brownian closure, as well as stochastic modified equations, weak expansions, B-series, cumulant expansions, state-dependent noise, and mean-square stability.

The final v1 claim is intentionally narrow: a descriptor-sufficiency study based on an analytical counterexample, controlled prediction tests, and explicit negative results.

Not claimed

  • Universal neural-network SGD spectral law
  • Universal critical batch-size formula
  • New general theory of state-dependent noise
  • Optimizer or generalization improvement
  • General moving-PGD / long-horizon closure theorem
Status

Why I stopped here

At this point the main claims are clear, the important negative results are documented, and the next questions are genuinely new work rather than cleanup. A moving-reference theorem, long-horizon remainder control, multidimensional transfer, or a new prospective neural test would belong to a separate v2.
