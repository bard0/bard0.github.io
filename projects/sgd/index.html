Finite-Step Stochastic Dynamics of SGD

A falsification-driven project on what stochastic information is actually needed to predict finite-step SGD stability and dynamics. The project evolved from spectral perturbation ideas to a sharper descriptor-sufficiency question, combining exact constructions, controlled numerical tests, and preregistered negative results.

Research question

From “does noise shift the spectrum?” to “what information is sufficient?”

The project began by treating stochasticity as a perturbation of local dynamics. Repeated controls and self-falsification gradually changed the scientific target. The final question is not whether another formal correction term exists, but which stochastic descriptors are sufficient for a stated finite-step observable, horizon, and order.

(Pηf)(θ) = E[f(θ − ηgB(θ))]

Research arc

How the project changed

1. Controlled spectral perturbationIn a stochastic Duffing testbed, the perturbative predictor tracked measured spectral shifts extremely well when the assumptions were deliberately enforced.
2. Additive-noise falsificationZero-mean additive noise did not provide a generic physical mean spectral-shift mechanism. Noise amplitude alone was not enough.
3. Temporal covariance hypothesisA proposed universal centered temporal-covariance “memory” mechanism failed the filtration audit for ordinary iid / with-replacement SGD and was downgraded.
4. State-dependent noise geometryAn exact scalar finite-sum construction showed that lower-order local descriptors can match while finite-step stability changes.
5. Higher-order neural transferA measurable mixed fourth derivative descriptor produced a small pooled gain but failed the frozen cross-regime robustness criteria.
6. Final framingThe mature project became a study of descriptor sufficiency: what information is required for a specified finite-step SGD target?
Analytical result

Low-order local descriptors can be insufficient

For an exactly solvable scalar finite-sum model, state-dependent stochastic geometry enters the exact second-moment stability boundary:

q = (1 − ηλ)² + η²GΣ
ηc = 2λ / (λ² + GΣ)

The contribution is an explicit descriptor-insufficiency statement: matching the lower-order reference geometry does not in general determine the finite-step stability target. The project does not claim that multiplicative noise or mean-square stability theory are themselves new.

Why the matched comparisons matter

The simpler projected-noise baseline already explains most absolute sharpness variation. The stronger evidence for state-dependent noise geometry appears when systems are matched on simpler descriptors and the target is the difference between them.

This is why the final claim is deliberately narrower than “noise geometry explains SGD.”

Negative result

A higher-order neural descriptor did not pass the robustness gate

Literature boundary

Positioning

The project is positioned relative to 2026 work on Edge-of-Stability dynamics and finite-step SGD beyond Brownian closure, as well as stochastic modified equations, weak expansions, B-series, cumulant expansions, state-dependent noise, and mean-square stability.

The final v1 claim is intentionally narrow: a descriptor-sufficiency investigation built from explicit analytical counterexamples, controlled predictive tests, and falsification.

Not claimed

  • Universal neural-network SGD spectral law
  • Universal critical batch-size formula
  • New general theory of state-dependent noise
  • Optimizer or generalization improvement
  • General moving-PGD / long-horizon closure theorem
Logical endpoint

Why v1 stops here

The portfolio objective is complete: the project shows hypothesis formation, solvable models, stochastic and spectral analysis, controlled validation, causal held-out design, literature comparison, explicit falsification, and claim narrowing. Further moving-reference theorems, long-horizon remainder control, multidimensional transfer, or new prospective neural tests are a separate publication-oriented v2.
