Finite-Step Stochastic Dynamics of SGD

Which local descriptors of minibatch noise determine finite-step mean-square stability and sharpness gaps in SGD? An exact finite-sum construction shows that matching lower-order descriptors need not fix the stability threshold when gradient-noise covariance depends on the state. Matched numerical comparisons test a state-dependent second-moment predictor; a pre-registered neural benchmark gives a complementary negative result for a fourth-order descriptor.

Mathematical setup

The minibatch transition operator

A finite SGD step acts on a test function through the Markov transition operator

(Pηf)(θ) = E[f(θ − ηgB(θ))]

Here θ is the parameter vector, η is the step size, B is the random minibatch, gB(θ) is its gradient estimate, f is a test function, and E averages over minibatch sampling. The question is which properties of the noise in gB determine a specified finite-step observable.

Study components

  • Exact analysis
    Finite-step mean-square stability in a scalar finite-sum model.
  • Controlled numerical studies
    Duffing spectral validation and matched-system predictions from a state-dependent second-moment model.
  • Neural benchmark
    A pre-registered negative test of a fourth-order descriptor.
Research path

Development of the research question

1. Controlled spectral perturbationIn a stochastic Duffing testbed, the perturbative predictor tracked measured spectral shifts very closely when the assumptions were enforced.
2. Additive-noise falsificationZero-mean additive noise did not provide a generic physical mean spectral-shift mechanism. Noise amplitude alone was not enough.
3. Temporal covariance hypothesisThe proposed centered temporal-covariance “memory” mechanism did not survive the filtration check for ordinary iid / with-replacement SGD.
4. State-dependent noise geometryAn exact scalar finite-sum construction showed that lower-order local descriptors can match while finite-step stability changes.
5. Higher-order neural transferThe mixed fourth derivative was measurable and gave a small pooled gain, but it failed the cross-regime criterion fixed before the final test.
6. Final questionThe project ended up as a study of descriptor sufficiency: what information is required for a specified finite-step SGD target?
Analytical result

Low-order local descriptors can be insufficient

For an exactly solvable scalar quadratic finite-sum model with state-dependent gradient-noise variance, the second-moment stability boundary is

q = (1 − ηλ)² + η²GΣ
ηc = 2λ / (λ² + GΣ)

Here q is the one-step second-moment contraction factor, λ > 0 is the curvature of the mean quadratic loss, η is the step size, and GΣ ≥ 0 is the state-dependent noise-variance coefficient. Mean-square stability requires q < 1; ηc is the resulting critical step size. Thus systems matched on simpler local descriptors can have distinct finite-step stability thresholds.

Why the matched comparisons matter

The simpler projected-noise baseline already explains most absolute sharpness variation. The stronger evidence for state-dependent noise geometry appears when systems are matched on simpler descriptors and the target is the difference between them.

So the claim is narrower than “noise geometry explains SGD”: the extra information matters in these matched finite-step comparisons.

Negative result

Negative result: a fourth-order neural descriptor

The pre-registered neural benchmark tested χmix = D⁴L[u,u,v,v] against a lower-order baseline using grouped held-out evaluation. Here L is the loss, D⁴L its fourth derivative, and u and v are the two evaluated parameter-space directions.

Scope and open problems

Scope and open problems

The study concerns descriptor sufficiency for specific finite-step observables: an exact scalar stability threshold, matched-system predictions, and a neural benchmark in which the fourth-order extension did not yield consistent improvement across learning-rate regimes. It sits alongside work on stochastic modified equations, weak expansions, state-dependent noise, and mean-square stability.

Natural theoretical extensions include a theorem for moving reference trajectories, control of long-horizon remainders, and multidimensional analogues of the exact construction. Further pre-registered neural tests could examine when covariance geometry transfers beyond the controlled models.
